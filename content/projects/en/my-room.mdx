This is the site you are reading, and it has no image files. Not a placeholder, not an avatar, not a social card. Everything you see was computed at render time from a string.

The string is usually a filename. `why-i-built-a-room` is hashed into a seed, the seed picks an ink set and a composition family, and out comes a picture. Because it is deterministic, the same article shows the same picture on the index, above the article, in search results and in its Open Graph card — four appearances, zero bytes stored.

## What it does

- Generates a unique illustration per post, project, gallery frame, section and social card, from a seed derived from that item's own name
- Picks between five composition families — arch, horizon, waves, orbit and shafts — so nothing repeats while everything stays in one series
- Renders as inline SVG on the server: no client JavaScript, infinite sharpness at any size, and nothing to lazy-load
- Runs the same engine on a canvas behind the hero, redrawn every frame, with light that follows the pointer

## What I learned

The hard part was not the maths. It was the first version, which produced sixteen variations of brown fog — because I had mixed every ink most of the way toward the ground colour and set the opacities low enough to be tasteful. Tasteful turned out to be a synonym for invisible. The fix was not more colour, it was more contrast: fewer, bolder shapes, and one bright thing per picture.

The second lesson was about restraint. My first attempt at the engine had nine parameters per composition and needed a week of tuning to stop looking random. The version that shipped has a small number of shapes placed by a small number of rules, and it looks deliberate because of what it leaves out.
